# Hodge Decomposition and Discrete Sector Splitting

## Status

- Classification: structural equivalence / operational analogy
- Scope: frozen discrete cochain, graph, and Laplacian regimes
- Claim ceiling: no physical-sector claim, no continuum derivation, no topology-as-ontology claim

## Candidate equivalence statement

In the discrete HAOS-IIP setting, the recurring exact / coexact / harmonic language is mathematically adjacent to the standard Hodge decomposition on cochain complexes.

The correspondence is:

`discrete sector split ≈ Hodge decomposition into exact / coexact / harmonic parts`

This is an operator-theoretic comparison. It says the repo’s sector bookkeeping behaves like a Hodge-style decomposition on finite complexes, not that HAOS-IIP has derived a physical gauge theory or continuum field law.

## Why this comparison is justified

The repository already uses this structure explicitly:

- exact-like content is associated with gradient or coboundary structure;
- coexact-like content is associated with transverse, circulation-like, or divergence-free structure;
- harmonic content is associated with persistent, topology-bearing residual structure;
- multiple frozen notes and phase documents already treat exact/coexact/harmonic as a sector split on discrete operators.

That makes Hodge decomposition the most direct mathematical comparison class.

## What is shared

- cochain-complex language
- Laplacian-based sector separation
- orthogonal decomposition into three structural parts
- harmonic remainder as a persistent global component
- exact/coexact bookkeeping under perturbation and refinement

## What is not shared

- no claim that the discrete sector split proves a continuum theorem
- no claim that the harmonic sector is a physical field by itself
- no claim that the split settles any external gauge, Maxwell, or topology claim
- no claim that the repository’s sector labels should be read as ontology rather than operator bookkeeping

## Evidence gates

The equivalence is only considered operationally valid if:

1. the exact / coexact / harmonic split is recoverable on the frozen operator family
2. the split survives the declared perturbation or refinement controls
3. the harmonic component remains distinct from exact leakage and coexact transport
4. the comparison does not collapse into a purely renaming exercise

If the split is not recoverable or control-separated, the note degrades to a descriptive analogy.

## Failure conditions

This note fails if any of the following happen:

- the equivalence is promoted to a physical-sector claim
- the harmonic component is treated as evidence of ontic topology
- the discrete split is used to justify a continuum derivation without a new contract
- control separation fails
- the note is used to reopen frozen bridge claims

## Audit summary

- Implemented fact: the repo already uses exact / coexact / harmonic sector language in frozen operator notes and experiments.
- Design choice: formalize that existing structure as a Hodge-style equivalence note.
- Heuristic: Hodge decomposition is the nearest standard mathematical comparison class.
- Analogy: the correspondence is structural, bounded, and discrete.
- Unverified hypothesis: the same language may be useful for future sector-recovery benchmarks if a frozen control suite is defined.
