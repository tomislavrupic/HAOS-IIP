# Discrete Curvature as Toy Gravity

## Status

- Classification: speculative analogy hunting
- Scope: frozen discrete curvature language mapped onto gravity-like language only as a thought experiment
- Claim ceiling: no physical claim, no derivation claim, no implication that discrete curvature is literal gravity

## Fun mapping

The playful mapping is:

`discrete curvature ↔ toy spacetime bending`

and, more specifically:

`transport bottleneck ↔ region of concentrated geometric resistance`

If local neighborhoods overlap strongly, the picture feels like a flat or
well-stitched region. If neighborhoods separate sharply, the picture feels like
an edge, bridge, or defect. That makes discrete curvature an easy toy language
for talking about "where geometry is hard to pass through."

## What feels similar

- positive curvature as a compressed neighborhood
- negative curvature as a bridge or saddle-like transport path
- Ricci-flow-like updates as a smoothing / redistribution story
- Cheeger-style bottlenecks as a rough proxy for geometric obstruction

## What stays different

- no claim of Einstein gravity
- no claim of spacetime dynamics
- no claim that the curvature signal is a metric tensor
- no claim that the analogy survives the frozen claim-gating rules

## Why it is interesting

The discrete curvature stack already behaves like a local-to-global geometry
probe. That makes it easy to imagine a toy gravity reading where matter-like
concentration shows up as transport resistance and flow-like updates act like a
very crude relaxation toward a smoother geometry.

## Boundary

This note is for speculation only.
If the analogy becomes useful, the next step is to ask whether there is any
frozen invariant that actually supports it.
