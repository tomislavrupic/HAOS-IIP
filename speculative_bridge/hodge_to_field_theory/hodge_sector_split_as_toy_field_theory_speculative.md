# Hodge Sector Split as Toy Field Theory

## Status

- Classification: speculative analogy hunting
- Scope: frozen Hodge / cochain language mapped onto field-theory language only as a thought experiment
- Claim ceiling: no physical claim, no derivation claim, no implication that the discrete sector split is literally a gauge field

## Fun mapping

The playful mapping is:

`exact / coexact / harmonic split ↔ toy field-theory bookkeeping`

More specifically:

- exact-like pieces feel like pure constraint or potential-like content
- coexact-like pieces feel like transverse or circulation-like content
- harmonic pieces feel like leftover physical-ish degrees of freedom

That is not a theorem. It is a bookkeeping metaphor.

## What feels similar

- cochain-complex language naturally separates pieces
- some parts look redundant, some constrained, some persistent
- the harmonic remainder feels like a candidate for "what survives"
- the language echoes gauge fixing without claiming gauge theory

## What stays different

- no claim of a real gauge field
- no claim of Maxwell theory
- no claim of continuum Lagrangians
- no claim that the Hodge split is itself a physical field equation

## Why it is interesting

The discrete Hodge picture already feels like a field theory in toy form:
you have a space of states, an operator that organizes them, and a residual
piece that behaves like the part you cannot get rid of by local reshuffling.

That makes it tempting to imagine the split as a proto-bookkeeping system for
constraints and degrees of freedom.

## Boundary

This note is for speculation only.
If the analogy becomes useful, the next step is to ask whether the field-theory
language merely paraphrases the Hodge split or reveals a separate invariant.
